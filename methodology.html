How we approach research

Evidence & methodology

Research evidence is easier to understand when study design, limitations and intended context are visible.

Start with the research question

A chemical profile can describe a molecule. That does not establish a meaningful benefit in people. Ask what the study actually measured, how it was conducted and which question the findings can answer.

Keep study types separate

Cell-culture experiments, animal models, observational studies and controlled human trials address different questions. A promising result in one setting does not automatically apply to another.

Look for uncertainty

Sample size, methods, measurement quality, publication date, replication and conflicts of interest all affect how evidence should be understood. Check whether a source describes limitations.

Read analytical information carefully

Purity, identity, formulation and stability are distinct concepts. A reported laboratory specification alone does not confirm fitness for a particular purpose, regulatory status or safe administration.

Regulatory context matters

Research-only materials and authorized medicines must not be treated as interchangeable. For up-to-date UK regulatory requirements, consult the relevant official regulator.
